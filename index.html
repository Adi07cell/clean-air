My research, simply

Smaller drops, more surface, faster transfer.

MDEA is an amine that can hold a lot of CO2, but it reacts slowly. I'm testing a Pickering emulsion: tiny drops of the amine solution, held steady by nanoparticles, so CO2 has far more surface to cross. Drag the slider.

0m² of drop surface per litre
1×vs. 2 mm drops
surface per volume = 6 × φ ÷ d  (drop fraction φ = 0.3)

Why it matters: absorption speed scales with the surface where gas and liquid meet. More surface attacks MDEA's weak point, its speed.

What I still check: whether the gain survives real plant costs, like extra pumping for a thicker liquid.
